About California

   California is known for Silicon Valley, Hollywood, stunning beaches, and innovative technology. It is the most populous state in the U.S. California is a diverse and influential U.S. state on the West Coast, known for its varied geography, massive economy, and global cultural impact. Its landscapes range from Pacific beaches and towering redwoods to the Sierra Nevada mountains, fertile Central Valley, and the deserts of the southeast, including Death Valley. Major cities such as Los Angeles, San Francisco, San Diego, Sacramento, and San Jose contribute to its prominence in entertainment, technology, agriculture, tourism, and innovation. California's culture is shaped by its multicultural population, strong ties to film and music, outdoor lifestyle, and a history that spans Indigenous civilizations, Spanish and Mexican periods, the Gold Rush, and later booms like Hollywood and Silicon Valley. Its climate varies from Mediterranean along the coast to desert and alpine regions inland, making the state both geographically and culturally unique.

Did You Know?

California's economy is so large that it ranks among the top in the world.


California is uniquely home to three world record trees, each remarkable in a different way. In Redwood National Park, the coast redwood called Hyperion holds the title of the tallest tree on Earth, stretching over 380 feet-taller than the Statue of Liberty. In Sequoia National Park, the giant sequoia named General Sherman is the largest tree in the world by volume, with a massive trunk that weighs an estimated 2.7 million pounds. And in the high, dry mountains of eastern California, the Ancient Bristlecone Pine Forest shelters some of the oldest living trees on the planet, including one bristlecone pine over 4,800 years old-older than the pyramids of Egypt. Together, these three record holders show just how biologically diverse California is, from foggy coasts to high-elevation deserts.
